DiscPop

Eject the disc, keep the drive.

Support Privacy Policy

DiscPop is a small menu bar app for Mac. It ejects the disc from your CD, DVD or Blu-ray drive, and only that disc. Your other drives are never touched.

Requirements: macOS 14 Sonoma or later, Apple silicon or Intel, and a CD, DVD or Blu-ray drive (USB or built-in).

Using DiscPop

Click the disc icon in your menu bar. You'll see your drive, its status and two buttons.

  • Eject Disc asks macOS to eject the disc safely. The drive stays connected.
  • Open Tray appears instead when the drive is empty, and opens the tray so you can load a disc.
  • Disconnect Drive ejects any disc and tells you when the drive is safe to unplug. macOS can't switch a USB drive off, so DiscPop never claims to.

If you have more than one optical drive connected, choose the drive from the menu at the top of the window.

Common questions

It says “Disc in Use”

macOS declined to eject the disc because something is using it. That could be an app with a file from the disc open, a Finder window, or macOS briefly reading a disc you just inserted. Close the app or wait a few seconds, then try again. DiscPop never forces a disc out.

A video player stopped when I ejected

Some players, such as VLC, read DVDs in a way macOS doesn't count as “in use”, so the eject goes ahead. Stop playback before you eject.

The tray won't close

Many slim USB drives have a spring-loaded tray that can't close by itself. Push it gently until it clicks shut.

My drive doesn't appear

Check the cable, and try a different USB port or a powered hub, since some drives need more power. If the drive works in Finder but not in DiscPop, please contact us with your drive's make and model.

Will DiscPop eject my other drives?

No. DiscPop only acts on CDs, DVDs and Blu-ray discs in the optical drive you've selected. USB sticks, external hard drives and SSDs never appear in DiscPop.

Can DiscPop burn, rip, play or change a drive's region?

No. DiscPop only ejects discs and opens trays.
